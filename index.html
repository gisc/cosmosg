Why this exists

Inspired by Jillian Lim’s CNA essay: everyday use and friendship can make learning a language feel less intimidating. The aim is connection, not another certificate.

Accuracy, pronunciation & confidence

This is a source-backed starter draft, not a native-speaker-reviewed phrasebook. High means a clear match in the cited phrase source, not a guarantee of Singapore usage. Medium means regional variety, register or reading needs speaker review. Low means an important element remains unverified.

Mandarin has pinyin. Cantonese uses the source's Yale-style readings, not Jyutping. Hokkien readings primarily follow a Taiwanese reference and must not be mistaken for a single Singapore standard. Teochew and Hakka retain source-specific spellings and tone numbers. Numbers and accents are not interchangeable across these systems. Some local-source readings are informal learning aids.

Hakka/Khek has multiple varieties; the reference does not specify a Singapore variety. All Hakka entries are marked Medium. The Teochew birthday pronunciation is deliberately withheld and marked Low. Tamil mixes clearly labelled spoken forms with formal forms; register needs local review. Vietnamese forms of address depend on age and relationship. Tagalog polite forms use po/ho.

No synthetic dialect audio is included. Use the recordings in the linked sources, or ask a speaker. A written pronunciation guide cannot teach all tones or tone changes.
